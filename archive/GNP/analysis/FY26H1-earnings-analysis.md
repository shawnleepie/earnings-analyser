### 1.3 Driver commentary

- **Revenue (+61% YoY / +28% HoH):** Almost entirely an Infrastructure-segment story (+93% YoY, +49% HoH) — HumeLink, TasNetworks NWTD, Western Power CELN mobilisation and the new Acciona/Western Renewables Link JV. Services and Energy & Engineering are secondary contributors.
- **Energy & Engineering — recurring HoH softness masked by YoY framing:** Revenue and profit are both **down** half-on-half (−5.8% revenue, −22.5% profit) for the **second consecutive comparable half** — the identical pattern flagged for this segment's predecessor ("Industrial Services") at FY25H1. H2 appears structurally the stronger half for this segment (BESS/EPC billing skew). The company's own materials present only YoY comparisons here ("Energy and Engineering... $151.5m, up 62% on PCP") — the HoH softening is nowhere in the company's own framing, for the second period running.
- **Margin — genuine improving trend, but a HoH wrinkle:** Statutory EBITDA margin expanded YoY (7.91%→8.64%), reversing the margin-dilution dynamic flagged at FY25H1 (where revenue growth of 33% materially outpaced normalised EBITDA growth of 25%). But margin actually **compressed HoH** (9.27%→8.64% statutory; 9.56%→8.65% normalised) — consistent with the Energy & Engineering softness above, since revenue grew faster HoH (+28.0%) than EBITDA (+19.3%/+15.7%). Neither the results-announcement nor the IR deck discusses margin at all this half — commentary is entirely $ and % growth-rate framed, a continuation of the pattern since FY25.
- **NPAT margin improved (4.12%→4.65%) despite an unfavourable finance-cost swing:** Net finance position reverted to a net **expense** ($82,758) from HY25's net **income** ($660,589) — exactly the reversal the FY25H1 file's own flag anticipated ("unlikely to repeat... as cash was progressively deployed into acquisitions/capex"). Finance costs rose 136% YoY on a much larger lease base ($57.9m vs $27.9m). That NPAT margin improved anyway means the operating-leverage gain below EBITDA more than offset this ~$743k headwind — a genuine quality point in the company's favour this half.
- **Tone/framing shift vs prior periods:** FY26H1's results-announcement.pdf (read in full) is the most confidently and repetitively "record"-framed release in the archive — five separate "Record" bullet points (revenue, EBITDA, NPAT, orderbook implied) plus MD language ("delivered **exceptional** results," "tailwinds... are **significant**"). This is a step up from FY25 (no results-announcement.pdf could even be located/retrieved) and from FY25H1 (image-only IR deck, materially thinner commentary). New this half: an explicit, forward-looking M&A signal — **"We remain receptive towards further M&A opportunities to continue our growth trajectory"** — not present in any prior period's outlook language, paired with an integration-comfort claim — **"Genus continues to bed down the acquisitions undertaken during FY2025, with... integration and synergies... proceeding well with expectations being met"** — which stands in some tension with FY25's own flagged acquired-debtor ECL spike (>16x) and the fact that PPA accounting for several FY25 acquisitions was still being finalised inside this very half (Note 21).
- **Strategy/execution tension flagged for cross-reference:** MD commentary repeats the long-standing "growing the east coast market" strategic language verbatim, yet this half's actual mix moved the other way — West Coast revenue +65% YoY vs East Coast +22% YoY, with East Coast's share of group revenue falling from 42% (FY25 full year) to 36% (H1FY26). Not a precise like-for-like (half vs full year denominators) but the direction is consistent and is cross-referenced in the disclosure-tone analysis (Section 4/5) below.

### 1.5 Guidance analysis

**Guidance:** "circa 35% growth in reported Normalised EBITDA in FY2026" — **reiterated**, not raised, this half (the upgrade from FY25's original 20–25% range to "circa 35%" was announced via a market update on 22 January 2026, pre-dating this release).

**Bridge from FY25 base and exit run-rate:**
- FY25 full-year Normalised EBITDA (base): $67,371,046
- Implied FY26 target at "circa 35%": ≈$90,950,912
- H1FY26 actual Normalised EBITDA: $46,287,725 (50.9% of the implied full-year target already banked)
- **Implied H2FY26 required: ≈$44,663,187** — this is *lower* in absolute dollars than H1FY26 itself, and versus H2FY25's derived base of $40,006,128, it implies **H2FY26 need only grow ≈+11.6% YoY** to hit the guide — a dramatic deceleration from H1FY26's own +69.1% YoY EBITDA-N growth.

**Puts and takes:**
- *Upside:* record orderbook $2.4bn (+20% since Jun-25); recurring revenue guided +20% in FY26 (from $311m FY25 base); tendered pipeline $2.6bn; new $429m syndicated facility and expanded $515m guarantee/surety headroom support continued mobilisation; established 3-year track record of guiding low and materially beating (FY24: 10–15% guided / 23.2% delivered; FY25: ≥20% guided / 48.6% delivered).
- *Downside/watch:* Energy & Engineering segment has now shown HoH revenue **and** profit softness for two consecutive comparable halves — the segment most exposed if the seasonal H2-strength pattern doesn't repeat a third time; FY26 capex guided sharply higher ($40–45m vs $34.4m net capex this half) which could pressure cash conversion even if earnings targets are met; new $429m facility terms (drawn amount, margin, tenor) remain entirely undisclosed; the growing unexplained "Other losses" charge (QoE item 4) is a small but real drag risk if it continues to scale.
- *Consensus comparison:* not available — no basis to assess beat/miss/in-line versus market expectations at time of release.

**Asymmetry call:** the guidance mathematically requires only a mild deceleration-to-modest-growth H2 (+11.6% YoY) against a H1 that grew 69% YoY — a very low bar relative to delivered momentum, orderbook visibility, and GNP's own three-year pattern of conservative guide-then-beat. **The setup looks skewed to the upside**: the realistic risk is a beat (as in FY24 and FY25), with the main identifiable downside risk being a further, larger HoH deterioration in Energy & Engineering than the two prior comparable halves have shown.

### 5.3 Market reaction view

*(View, not a prediction — no consensus data exists this session to benchmark an actual beat/miss, which is the primary caveat on everything below.)*

On the numbers alone, this is a strong, broad-based result: every statutory profitability line grew 60–90% YoY, cash conversion improved on the one metric comparable across the full archive, net cash roughly doubled YoY, and the company introduced its first-ever interim dividend — all supportive of a positive market reaction if read at face value. Two things could temper an otherwise unambiguously positive reaction, and constitute the asymmetric-setup flag this framework calls for:
- **Quality-of-earnings and disclosure-integrity readers** (analysts who go past the headline release into the financial statements) will find two unresolved, material internal inconsistencies in the FY26H1 accounts themselves (inventory comparator, contract-liabilities comparator) plus a growing unexplained "Other losses" charge, a JV disclosure regression, and a $429m facility with no disclosed terms. None of these change the headline profit result, but collectively they are the kind of finding that shows up in a "quality of earnings looks worse than the headline" note days after the initial move, not on the day itself.
- **The Energy & Engineering HoH softening is invisible in the company's own framing** (YoY-only commentary) and would only surface to a market participant building their own segment model — a second consecutive half of this pattern raises the probability it is treated as guidance risk by the more careful analysts, even though management calls the segment's result "better than expected."

**Net view:** the immediate/headline market reaction is more likely positive than negative given the magnitude of the YoY beats and the maiden dividend, but the setup has a **modest asymmetric skew where the risk is concentrated in second-order scrutiny** (accounts-level data-quality issues, undisclosed debt terms, and a masked segment softening) rather than in the headline numbers themselves — a "buy the headline, then find the footnotes" pattern rather than a clean, one-directional beat.
